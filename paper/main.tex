\begin{abstract}
We propose a controlled study of whether reference-relative sparse-region targeting adds value to otherwise matched quality-diversity search. The initial executable testbed searches a bounded expression language for online bin-packing policies. Both arms share generation, archive design, resource allowances, and evaluation; only parent-selection weights differ. Objective utility, behavioral diversity, and scientific novelty are treated as separate outcomes. The current reference screen cannot establish positive novelty and returns unresolved judgments for non-reference policies. Confirmatory experiments, a broader prior-art corpus, and independent mechanism-level adjudication remain future work. No superiority or validated-discovery claim is made in this proposal.
\end{abstract}
\section{Research Question}
Does sparse-region targeting improve independently validated discovery yield over matched quality-diversity search? The present pilot examines a narrower behavioral-policy version of this question.
\section{Related Work}
Novelty search and MAP-Elites establish explicit exploration and quality-diversity objectives \citep{lehman2011abandoning,mouret2015illuminating}. ELM and LLMatic already combine language models with QD \citep{lehman2022elm,nasir2023llmatic}. FunSearch and AlphaEvolve maintain program diversity during executable search \citep{romera2024mathematical,alphaevolve2025}. EoH and BehaveSim are close comparators for heuristic design and behavioral similarity \citep{liu2024eoh,zhang2026behavesim}. Independent novelty measurement needs evidence-based evaluation \citep{novelty2025sdp,rqbench2026}.
\section{Pilot Method}
A common bounded JSON policy language, reference corpus, probe panel, and QD archive are used in both arms. Parent sampling is uniform or weighted by reference-relative behavioral sparsity. Full definitions and budgets are versioned in the repository protocol.
\section{Evaluation and Evidence Status}
Pilot outcomes must be generated from recorded artifacts. Any engineering results are descriptive and do not establish the primary novelty hypothesis. An unresolved novelty verdict is not converted into a positive label. Failed and incomplete comparisons are preserved.
\section{Limitations}
The policy language excludes general code generation, the instance distributions are synthetic, and the reference set is small. Fixed-probe distances are not algorithmic-equivalence proofs. Hosted-model training contamination and stochasticity are not eliminated by retrieval restrictions or local seeds. Generalization and practical effect margins require further study.
\section{Research Integrity}
Negative and inconclusive findings are eligible for reporting. Claims will be revised to match evidence, with protocol amendments and failed runs retained.
